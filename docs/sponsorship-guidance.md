## Sponsorship Guidance for Chapter Leads

Sponsors and community partners can play an important role in helping Global Security Community chapters run accessible, high-quality events. Sponsorship should help reduce event costs, keep events free or low-cost for attendees, and support practical community outcomes such as catering, venue, AV, signage, accessibility, speaker support and event operations.

Sponsorship should always align with the values of the Global Security Community: community-first, inclusive, practical, educational and respectful.

### Sponsorship Principles

Chapter Leads should approach sponsorship as community support, not as a sales or lead-generation activity.

Sponsors are welcomed as supporters of the community, but the integrity of the event should remain with the Chapter Lead and organising team.

When considering sponsorship, Chapter Leads should ensure:

* The sponsor is aligned with the purpose and values of the Global Security Community.
* The sponsorship helps support genuine event costs or attendee experience.
* The event remains community-led and educational.
* Sponsor recognition is appropriate, transparent and proportionate.
* Attendee privacy is protected.
* The agenda is not influenced by sponsorship.
* Any sponsor engagement with attendees is voluntary and opt-in.

### What Sponsorship Can Support

Sponsor contributions may be used to support practical event costs, including:

* Venue hire
* Catering and refreshments
* Coffee or drinks
* AV and technical equipment
* Event signage
* Name badges and lanyards
* Printing
* Accessibility needs
* Speaker or volunteer support
* Photography or video
* Community promotion
* Post-event engagement

Where possible, Chapter Leads should estimate expected costs before approaching sponsors. This helps keep sponsorship requests clear and proportionate.

### Suggested Sponsorship Models

For most chapter events, especially smaller or first-time events, sponsorship should be kept simple.

A small event does not need multiple complex sponsorship tiers. A simpler model is easier to manage, easier for sponsors to understand and better aligned with a community-first approach.

Example sponsorship options:

| Sponsorship option | Purpose                                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Event Supporter    | General contribution towards event costs such as signage, badges, printing and operations       |
| Catering Sponsor   | Supports food and refreshments for attendees                                                    |
| Coffee Sponsor     | Supports coffee, drinks or informal networking                                                  |
| Community Partner  | Provides in-kind support such as venue, AV, photography, printing, swag or volunteer assistance |

For larger events, Chapter Leads may choose to introduce tiered sponsorship options such as Bronze, Silver or Gold. This should only be done where the event size, budget and sponsor demand justify the additional administration.

### Sponsor Recognition

Sponsor recognition should be clear, fair and appropriate for the level of support provided.

Recognition may include:

* Logo placement on the event page
* Logo placement on event holding slides
* Social media thank-you posts
* Acknowledgement during opening or closing remarks
* Sponsor table, subject to venue availability
* Logo placement near catering or coffee areas, where relevant
* Opportunity to provide community-appropriate swag
* Opportunity to run an opt-in prize draw

Sponsor recognition should not make the event feel sales-led or reduce the focus on community learning.

### Sponsorship Boundaries

To protect the integrity of Global Security Community events, the following boundaries are recommended:

* Sponsorship does not guarantee a speaking slot.
* Sponsors should submit sessions through the same Call for Speakers process as other speakers.
* Sponsor sessions should be selected based on relevance, quality and community value.
* Sales-led or product-pitch sessions should not be included in the main agenda.
* Attendee contact lists must not be provided to sponsors.
* Sponsor follow-up with attendees should only occur where attendees have clearly opted in.
* Prize draws, QR code scans or marketing sign-ups must be voluntary.
* Sponsors must respect the event Code of Conduct.
* Sponsor tables or booths should be subject to venue capacity and event format.

### Speaking Opportunities for Sponsors

Sponsors are welcome to contribute to the community by submitting technical, educational or experience-based sessions.

Sponsor submissions should be reviewed through the normal selection process. Suitable sessions may include:

* Real-world implementation lessons
* Technical demos
* Case studies
* Security architecture patterns
* Lessons learned from incidents or uplift projects
* Practical guidance that attendees can apply

Sessions that are primarily product demonstrations, sales presentations or marketing-led should be avoided unless clearly labelled as sponsor content and kept separate from the main community agenda.

### Attendee Privacy

Chapter Leads must protect attendee privacy.

Attendee lists, email addresses and registration details should not be shared with sponsors.

Sponsors may collect attendee details only through voluntary, opt-in methods, such as:

* QR codes at a sponsor table
* Optional prize draw forms
* Direct attendee conversations
* Sponsor-hosted opt-in forms

Any data collected by sponsors is the responsibility of the sponsor and should be clearly explained to attendees at the point of collection.

### In-Kind Sponsorship

In-kind support can be just as valuable as financial sponsorship, particularly for smaller chapters or first-time events.

Examples of in-kind support include:

* Venue space
* Catering
* Coffee cart or drinks
* AV equipment
* Photography
* Printing
* Name badges
* Swag
* Speaker gifts
* Volunteer support

Chapter Leads should confirm what is being provided, who is responsible for delivery and any conditions attached to the support.

### Sponsor Approval and Suitability

Before accepting sponsorship, Chapter Leads should consider whether the sponsor is appropriate for the community and the event.

Consider:

* Is the sponsor relevant to the security or technology community?
* Does the sponsor align with the Global Security Community values?
* Are there any conflicts of interest?
* Is the sponsor expecting benefits that are not appropriate for a community event?
* Are there any reputational concerns?
* Are the sponsorship terms clear and manageable?

Where unsure, Chapter Leads should seek guidance from the Global Security Community leadership team before confirming sponsorship.

### Financial and Administrative Considerations

Before confirming a financial sponsorship, Chapter Leads should clarify:

* Who will issue the invoice
* Who will receive the funds
* Whether tax, GST, VAT or local equivalents apply
* Whether sponsorship amounts are inclusive or exclusive of tax
* Whether the chapter or central community body is responsible for payment processing
* What payment deadline applies
* Whether any local legal, financial or reporting requirements apply

Chapter Leads should avoid personally receiving or managing sponsorship funds unless this has been explicitly approved through the appropriate Global Security Community process.

### Sponsor Assets and Deadlines

Sponsors should provide required assets by an agreed deadline.

Common sponsor assets include:

* High-resolution logo, preferably SVG or PNG
* Sponsor website URL
* Short company description
* Preferred social media handles
* Contact person for event coordination
* Details of any swag, prize draw or sponsor table requirements

Chapter Leads should confirm where and how sponsor logos will be used before publishing.

### Suggested Sponsor Communication

When responding to sponsor interest, Chapter Leads may use wording similar to:

“Thank you for your interest in supporting our Global Security Community event. Sponsorship helps us keep the event accessible and supports practical costs such as catering, venue, AV, signage and event operations. We keep sponsorship community-focused, with appropriate recognition such as logo placement, social media thanks and event acknowledgement. Sponsorship does not include guaranteed speaking slots or attendee contact lists, and any attendee engagement should be voluntary and opt-in.”

### Post-Event Sponsor Follow-Up

After the event, Chapter Leads should thank sponsors and provide a short summary of the event.

Post-event follow-up may include:

* Thank-you email
* Public social media acknowledgement
* Event attendance summary
* Photos, where appropriate and permitted
* Link to published slides or recordings
* Feedback summary
* Invitation to support future events

Sponsors should be acknowledged for helping support the community, not positioned as owning or controlling the event.

### Recommended Approach

For most chapters, the recommended approach is:

1. Estimate event costs first.
2. Keep sponsorship options simple.
3. Prioritise catering, venue, AV and attendee experience.
4. Keep sponsor benefits clear and proportionate.
5. Do not share attendee lists.
6. Do not guarantee speaking slots.
7. Keep the event community-led.
8. Thank sponsors publicly and professionally.

Sponsorship should make the event better for attendees while preserving the independence, trust and community-first nature of Global Security Community events.
